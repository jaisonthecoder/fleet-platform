1 · System context

Who talks to what. CargoWise is the system of record; the portal is a customer window onto it plus a channel for customer requests back into it.

The portal never queries CargoWise on a customer keystroke. Every screen is served from its own synchronised projection, and every external system sits behind an adapter interface — so changing the mechanism for one flow changes one adapter and no business logic.

2 · How we classify an exchange

The question that decides the mechanism for every flow in this pack.

Your Snowflake extraction is the natural answer for the analytical and bulk class, and very possibly for the initial backfill of the operational class. It is not an answer for the transactional class, and on its own it is unlikely to meet the freshness target for milestone visibility.

flowchart TD
  Q1{"Does one person perform a single
business act that must not be lost
or duplicated?"}
  Q1 -- yes --> T["TRANSACTIONAL
request and response, or an inbound
message with an idempotency key
and a business acknowledgement"]
  Q1 -- no --> Q2{"Does freshness
matter in minutes?"}
  Q2 -- yes --> O["OPERATIONAL STREAM
event-driven push, with polling
or an extract as fallback"]
  Q2 -- no --> Q3{"A large historical set,
or reference data?"}
  Q3 -- "large history" --> AN["ANALYTICAL AND BULK
scheduled extract, or a replicated
dataset such as Snowflake"]
  Q3 -- "reference data" --> R["REFERENCE
periodic extract, maintained
in the portal thereafter"]
  T -.- W1["wrong answer:
a nightly file"]
  O -.- W2["wrong answer: a synchronous
call on every page view"]
  AN -.- W3["wrong answer: replaying a message
stream from the beginning"]

3 · Tenant resolution — the rule everything depends on

How an inbound CargoWise record is assigned to the right customer, and the four gates it must pass before any person sees it.

This is why we ask whether a party on a shipment or an invoice carries the organisation code or the address code. The schema shows both are optional, so a party can arrive with neither. One real shipment message with a party block answers it completely.

  Q -- no --> UN["Stored, visible to nobody.

of an un-onboarded customer"]
  Q -- yes --> G1{"Gate 1 ELIGIBLE

  G1 -- no --> DENY["Not visible"]

single-office restriction"}
  G3 -- no --> DENY
  G3 -- yes --> G4{"Gate 4 ALLOWED
does the user hold the permission,
and is the feature switched on?"}
  G4 -- no --> DENY
  G4 -- yes --> SHOW["Visible to that user"]

5 · Quotation, end to end

The first capability we deliver, and the only place the portal writes commercial content into CargoWise.

No rate management exists today, so every quote is priced by a person, largely in email and spreadsheets. The portal standardises that work. Note the gate: the customer sees no price until CargoWise has confirmed the write-back. Pricing in CargoWise stays fully supported and nobody has to change how they work.

  participant C as Customer user
  participant P as Portal
  participant Q as Pricing user
  participant A as Approver
  participant CW as CargoWise

  Note over P: Organisation code resolved server side,
never supplied by the client
  P->>CW: Create quote request, idempotent
on the portal reference
  P->>Q: Request enters the branch-scoped queue

  Q->>P: Charge lines, totals, validity,
carrier, service, transit

    Q->>A: Submit for approval
    A->>P: Approve, or reject with a reason
  end

on quote and version
  CW-->>P: Business acceptance
  Note over P: The customer sees nothing
until this confirmation arrives
  P->>C: Priced quote, branded, with PDF
  C->>P: Accept within validity,
or reject with a reason code
  P->>CW: Accept, reject or cancel

6 · Quote lifecycle

The states a quote passes through, including the one the customer cannot see.

A failed write to CargoWise holds the quote in Pending sync, alerts operations and is retried. The customer never sees an error, and never sees a price CargoWise has not accepted.

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Draft
  Draft --> Requested
  Requested --> InProgress
  InProgress --> AwaitingApproval
  AwaitingApproval --> InProgress
  AwaitingApproval --> PendingSync
  InProgress --> PendingSync
  PendingSync --> Quoted
  PendingSync --> SyncFailed
  SyncFailed --> PendingSync
  Quoted --> Accepted
  Quoted --> Rejected
  Quoted --> Expired
  Requested --> Cancelled
  Quoted --> Cancelled
  Accepted --> [*]

7 · Quote, booking and shipment — three entities, not a chain

How the portal models the commercial objects.

CargoWise does not convert a quote into a booking. A booking is linked to a quote by quote number, one to many, and is separately converted into a shipment. The portal carries the quote number and lets CargoWise establish the link. A booking may exist with its shipment reference arriving later, or never.

8 · Booking, end to end

From an accepted quote, or directly without one. Both routes are supported.

Change and cancellation are blocked once cargo has been picked up or delivered to an off-site location, with a hard stop at the Shipped event. Enforced on the server, and configurable.

9 · Track and trace — one capability, two exchanges

Historical backfill and ongoing change have different volumes, different freshness needs and different right answers.

This is the direct answer to the question of showing a customer all their shipments for the last year. It is never a live call into CargoWise — it is a read of our projection, populated once by backfill and then kept current by change. Design target: list and search at 100,000 rows per customer, list under two seconds.

10 · Milestone event pipeline

The highest-volume flow in the platform, sized for millions of events per month.

Events use an exclude-list: show everything except a short flagged set. An unmapped code is stored but never displayed, and raised for classification. Air milestones must be published within three hours of actual occurrence for the Cargo IQ commitment; our internal target is sixty seconds from CargoWise.

  PUB --> VAR["ETA variance against original
and last known"]
  VAR --> ALERT["Potential delay alert"]

11 · Document visibility — an allow-list

Only explicitly approved document types ever reach a customer.

Document type in CargoWise is assigned manually by the uploading user and is not validated. A mistagged rated bill of lading would expose freight rates to a customer. So anything unmapped, newly introduced or of unknown type stays hidden until somebody classifies it. We already hold the 479-code catalogue; the approved list is owed by Noatum operations.

Single and mass download"]

12 · Financial visibility — the addressee rule

Invoices resolve from the addressee organisation on the transaction, never from the shipment parties.

Who is billed depends on Incoterms and the commercial agreement, with no single fixed rule. Two parties on one shipment can each receive their own invoice, and cross-trade billing means a third party such as the notify party can be the paying customer. The addressee field is therefore load-bearing for us.

  INV["Invoice or credit note

  MATCH -- yes --> PERM{"User holds the

  PERM -- yes --> OFFICE{"User restricted
to one office?"}
  OFFICE -- yes --> ONE["Only that office documents"]
  OFFICE -- no --> ALL["All the customer
financial documents"]

13 · Alerting and delay detection

Most alerting is ours to compute. No new CargoWise exchange is required.

Carriers do not reliably send a revised ETA, and detection is manual today, so the portal computes ETA variance itself. Customs holds already reach CargoWise and today trigger an email to an operator who then telephones the customer; surfacing the hold automatically removes that work.

16 · Delivery sequence
